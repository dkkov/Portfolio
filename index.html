Project

Nordwell came to me with 4,200 support tickets a month across five channels, an eleven-hour first response time and a knowledge base nobody trusted. We rebuilt the entire intake layer around an AI agent that classifies, drafts and resolves — and escalates the second confidence drops.

Eight months in, 71% of tickets close without a human, first response sits under four minutes, and the same headcount handles three times the order volume. Every answer traces back to a source document, and a weekly eval run catches drift before customers ever feel it.

I'm an automation architect, systems thinker and stubborn simplifier, driven by the gap between how a process is described and how it actually runs. My work lives where clear thinking meets ruthless tooling. I spent nine years inside operations — logistics, e-commerce support, back-office finance — before I ever touched an LLM. That's why my projects start with a whiteboard and a stopwatch, never with a prompt.

Lumen Logistics Verta Clinic Kastl Mfg

Today I design and ship AI-driven automations for teams of ten to five hundred people. The good ones are invisible: tickets answer themselves, invoices reconcile overnight, the Monday report writes itself. Outside of work I'm endlessly curious about how people actually behave around systems they didn't ask for — which is why I spend as much time on training and change management as on code. Success isn't how clever the system is. It's how many hours nobody has to spend anymore.
